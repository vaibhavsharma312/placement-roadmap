Campus placement preparation: a 12-week roadmap for engineering students

If placements are three months away and you do not know where to start, this plan splits your time between DSA, CS fundamentals, aptitude, your resume and mock interviews.

Written by Vaibhav Sharma, M.Tech student. Last updated 13 November 2026.

The 12-week campus placement preparation roadmap

Spend about two to three focused hours on weekdays and a longer block on weekends. Adjust the weeks to your own calendar.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: Foundations

    Pick one language (C++, Java or Python) and get fluent with arrays, strings and basic complexity analysis. Solve easy problems daily.

  2. Weeks 3 to 5: Core data structures

    Linked lists, stacks, queues, hashing, recursion and sorting. Write each structure by hand at least once.

  3. Weeks 6 to 8: Trees, graphs and dynamic programming

    Binary trees, BST, BFS and DFS, then the common DP patterns. Move to medium-level problems.

  4. Weeks 9 to 10: CS fundamentals and aptitude

    Revise operating systems, DBMS and SQL, computer networks and OOP. Add timed aptitude and logical reasoning sets.

  5. Weeks 11 to 12: Mock interviews and polish

    Give timed mock interviews, rehearse explaining your projects aloud, and finalise your resume.

DSA for placements: what to prioritise

Most online assessments and technical rounds repeat the same families of problems. Cover these first.

CS fundamentals to revise

  • Operating systems: processes, threads, scheduling, deadlocks
  • DBMS: normalisation, transactions, joins and indexing
  • Computer networks: the TCP/IP model, HTTP, DNS
  • OOP: the four pillars with real examples

Resume and projects that get shortlisted

Keep your resume to one page. Lead with projects you can explain end to end, and write each bullet as an action with a result, for example "reduced query time by indexing the orders table".

Be honest about your own contribution

Interviewers ask follow-up questions. List only what you built yourself and be clear about what came from a library, tutorial or teammate.

Mock interviews

Practise with a friend or senior, and explain your thinking out loud. Communication is scored as much as the final answer.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a day should I study for placements?

Two to three consistent hours on weekdays works better than occasional long sessions. Consistency matters more than volume.

How many DSA problems are enough?

There is no magic number. Aim for understanding the patterns behind roughly 150 to 250 well-chosen problems rather than racing through a huge count.

Can I prepare for placements while doing a thesis or projects?

Yes. Keep a fixed daily slot for practice and treat your thesis or projects as source material for interview discussions.
